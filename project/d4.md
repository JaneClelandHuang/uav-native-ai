# Project Deliverable 4 — Testing Agent + Engineering Evidence

> **DRAFT ONLY — not yet reviewed. Details, dates, and grading below

## Objectives

Build an agent that can tell you whether your system does what you said
it would, and find out how far you can trust its answer.

By the end of this deliverable, your team will have:

- An **oracle table** that turns your D1 success criteria and use-case
  extensions into checks that can fail.
- A **testing agent** that knows your project, runs those checks at the
  right layer, judges the results, and reports with evidence.
- Run it against **randomly injected faults** in your running system, and
  learned what it catches, what it misses, and when it cries wolf.

**Native AI focus — Trusting an Agent's Verdict.** In HW5 you asked "can AI
write tests?" Here the question is harder: when an agent says your system
works, or doesn't, how do you know it's right? Generating tests is easy.
Knowing what a correct result is takes your intent: the success criteria,
use case and ADRs you've written since D1.

---

## How This Differs From HW5

| | HW5 test skill | D4 testing agent |
|---|---|---|
| **Where "correct" comes from** | The code under test | Your documented intent: success criteria, use-case extensions, ADRs |
| **What it does** | Writes tests; you run them | Runs the loop itself: sets up a scenario, observes, judges, reports |
| **What it tests** | One component | The whole system: several drones, timing, the person in the GUI, and an AI component whose answers vary |
| **How it's judged** | Do the tests look sensible? | What it catches and misses when random faults are injected |

Start from your team's HW5 skills: compare them, keep the best, and build
from there.

### Using AI

Use AI however you like: this deliverable *is* an AI agent. **Every result
your agent reports, someone on your team has checked** before you rely on
it, and that includes its passes. An agent that generates hundreds of
tests with no oracle is AI slop in test form. **You are entirely
responsible for what you present**, whether AI helped make it or not.

### What you build

#### 1. The oracle table

In `docs/verification/oracles.md`: **at least 6 oracles** (4 for teams of
two), each one a row:

| Column | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Source | The success criterion, use-case step or extension, ADR, or guarantee it comes from |
| Oracle | What must be true, stated so it can fail: *a drone with no telemetry for 2 s is marked stale* |
| Layer | Unit, integration, system scenario, or GUI/operator |
| Scenario | How the agent sets it up and observes it |
| Last result | Pass or fail, when, and a link to the evidence |

Cover the layers:

| Layer | What it checks | Where it runs |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit** | Pure logic: validator, world model, parsers | CI, on every PR |
| **Integration** | One component plus the broker: the right topics and messages in and out | Your machine, against the fleet |
| **System scenario** | A use case end to end, several drones, the AI component included | Your machine, against the fleet |
| **GUI / operator** | The operator sees what they need, and their decision reaches the system | Your machine, checked at the message level |

At least one oracle must be a **system scenario** and at least one a
**GUI/operator** oracle. Unit tests keep running in CI as in D3.

**Your AI component answers differently from run to run.** An oracle that
touches it runs the scenario **several times** and judges the rate, as in
HW7: *the clue pipeline proposes `circle` for the teddy bear in at least 4
of 5 runs*.

#### 2. The testing agent

A skill at `.claude/skills/<your-agent>/SKILL.md`, plus any scripts it
uses, in your team repo. Given a request ("check the stale-drone oracle",
"check everything", "check what this PR could break"), it:

1. **Reads your project context:** `CLAUDE.md`, the oracle table, the use
   case and the relevant ADRs.
2. **Chooses the layer** and sets up the scenario: starts what it needs,
   publishes what it needs, waits and observes.
3. **Judges** each result against the oracle, not against what the code
   happens to do.
4. **Reports** in `reports/<date>-<what>.md`: each oracle checked, pass or
   fail, and the evidence (message traces, log excerpts, numbers, repeat
   counts).
5. **Updates** the "Last result" column of the oracle table.

Given a PR, it says **which oracles the change could affect, and why**, and
re-checks those.

It must **never change your system's code** to make a check pass. It
reports; people fix.

#### 3. What it got wrong

Before Dec. 3, document at least **one thing your agent got wrong** (a
missed failure, a false alarm, a misunderstood oracle) and what you changed
because of it, in your agent's folder (`README.md`).

#### 4. Git history

**At least 3 small, reviewed, merged PRs per team member** between
Thursday, Nov. 19 and Thursday, Dec. 3, each closing an issue and approved
by a teammate other than its author, and **no direct commits to `main`**.

### Fault runs (outside class, by Dec. 3)

Download [`mischief_d4.py`](tools/mischief_d4.py) and put it in your team
repo's root. Don't commit it.

Do **three runs**. For each one:

1. Start your system and a normal run of your use case.
2. Start the mischief maker: `python mischief_d4.py`. It waits for a drone
   to take off, then injects **one fault at a random moment, or possibly
   none at all**. It never touches your code, and it prints nothing about
   what it chose.
3. Run your agent on your oracles. Give it at most about 10 minutes.
4. Stop the mischief maker (Ctrl-C). It undoes the fault and writes a
   receipt, `mischief-receipt-<time>.txt`.
5. **Commit the agent's report and the receipt.**
6. Only then run `python mischief_d4.py --reveal <receipt>`, and add a
   short "Revealed" section to that report: what was injected, and whether
   your agent caught it, missed it, raised a false alarm, or blamed the
   wrong thing.

**What can be injected**, at the level of categories: a sensor fault (GPS,
vibration, compass, battery) on one drone, a drone whose backend goes
silent, a person detection far outside any search area, a malformed
message or impossible telemetry, or nothing.

**Honor system:** find out what happened only through your agent. Don't
read the receipt, the broker traffic or the simulator's parameters
yourself before you've committed the report. The commit order is part of
what we look at.

Saying "everything passed" when nothing was injected is a correct answer.
Saying it when something was injected is the most expensive mistake an
agent can make.
