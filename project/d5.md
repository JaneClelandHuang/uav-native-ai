# Project Deliverable 5 — Final System + Website

> **DRAFT ONLY — not yet reviewed. Details, dates, and grading below
> are subject to change before this is final.**

## Objectives

Show what you built doing its job, and make the engineering case for it.

By the end of the project, your team will have:

- Flown your use case live, end to end, with the person on the loop
  deciding in your GUI, and handled at least one thing going wrong.
- Watched someone outside your team operate your system, and learned
  where they struggled.
- A final website that tells the engineering story, from vision to
  evidence to honest limitations.

**Native AI focus — Owning the Result.** By now AI has written a lot of your
code, tests and documentation. The final question is the one the course
started with: what did you delegate to AI, what stayed a human
responsibility, and how did you decide whether the AI's work could be
trusted?

### Using AI

Use AI however you like. **Every deliverable must be vetted by a human on
your team.** We don't want AI slop: text generated in bulk, unread by its
authors, that everyone else is then expected to read. Every claim on your
website is one someone on your team has checked, and short beats long.
**You are entirely responsible for what you present**, whether AI helped
make it or not.

---

### Dress rehearsal (Thursday, Dec. 3)

Not graded, but expected. About **15 minutes per team**:

1. **Run your whole final demo**, in the time you'll have on Dec. 8, as if
   it were the real thing.
2. **A member of your partner team operates your GUI** for one run of your
   use case, with no coaching beyond what an operator would get. Watch
   where they hesitate, misread or get stuck, and write it down: it is the
   operator evidence for your website.
3. **Your partner team gives feedback** (3 minutes): what was unclear,
   what broke, what you should cut.

You have five days to act on it.

---

### Final live demo (Tuesday, Dec. 8)

About **15 minutes per team: 10 minutes of demonstration, then 5 minutes
of live questions.** The live system is the centerpiece. A title slide and
one slide of evidence are fine; more than that is not.

1. **Problem and vision** (about 1 minute).
2. **Live mission** (about 6 minutes): your use case end to end in the
   simulator, multiple drones, perceive → reason → act, with the person
   deciding in your GUI. **Show at least one extension handled live**: a
   drone going silent, a bad detection, a failed approval.
3. **Evidence** (about 2 minutes): what your testing agent checks, what
   it found, what it missed.
4. **Limitations** (about 1 minute): what doesn't work, and why.

Questions come from your partner team, the instructor and any guests.
Have a recording ready: if the live run fails, switch to it. You lose the
live-run points but not the rest.

---

### The final website (Monday, Dec. 14)

Your site has been growing since D1. Bring every page up to date, and add
what's new:

| Page | What it needs |
|---|---|
| **Home** | Project name, team, one-line vision, a short video of your system running |
| **Vision & Use Cases** | Updated from D1; what changed in your vision and why |
| **Architecture** | The final architecture, ADRs current (superseded ones kept), the GUI screens |
| **Implementation** | What works, what's mocked, what was cut (your D3 scope list, honestly updated) |
| **Verification & Evidence** | Your D4 oracle table with current results, the fault runs and what they revealed, what your agent missed, and the evidence behind your success criteria |
| **Operator evidence** | What happened when someone outside your team ran your use case in the GUI, and what you changed |
| **Native AI** | What engineering work you delegated to AI, what stayed a human responsibility, and how you decided whether the AI's work was trustworthy, with specific examples |
| **Reflection & Limitations** | What you'd do differently, the limitations a real deployment would hit, and anything that never worked |
