# Project Deliverable 5 — Final System + Website

> **DRAFT ONLY — not yet reviewed. Details, dates, and grading below
> are subject to change before this is final.**

## Objectives

Show what you built doing its job, and make the engineering case for it.

By the end of the project, your team will have:

- Flown your use case live, end to end, with the person on the loop
  deciding in your GUI, and handled at least one thing going wrong.
- Watched someone outside your team operate your system, and learned
  where they struggled.
- A final website that tells the engineering story, from vision to
  evidence to honest limitations.

**Native AI focus — Owning the Result.** By now AI has written a lot of your
code, tests and documentation. The final question is the one the course
started with: what did you delegate to AI, what stayed a human
responsibility, and how did you decide whether the AI's work could be
trusted?

### Using AI

Use AI however you like. **Every deliverable must be vetted by a human on
your team.** We don't want AI slop: text generated in bulk, unread by its
authors, that everyone else is then expected to read. Every claim on your
website is one someone on your team has checked, and short beats long.
**You are entirely responsible for what you present**, whether AI helped
make it or not.

---

### Team meeting (Thursday, Dec. 3)

Not graded, but every team member attends. Class meets in the **UG
Commons**; each team meets with the instructor for about **15 minutes**
while the others keep working. Bring your running system and your project
board, and be ready to go over:

1. **What's done:** show it running.
2. **What isn't:** against your D3 scope list. What is still mocked, broken
   or not started.
3. **The plan for Dec. 8:** what will be in your demo, what you're cutting,
   and the extension you'll show live.
4. **Your D4 results:** what your agent found in the fault runs, and what
   it missed.

You leave with an agreed scope for Tuesday.

---

### Final live demo (Tuesday, Dec. 8)

**15 minutes per team, then 5 minutes of questions.** The live system is
the centerpiece. A few slides for context and evidence are fine; the
mission itself is shown running, not on slides.

1. **Problem and vision** (about 2 minutes).
2. **Live mission** (about 8 minutes): your use case end to end in the
   simulator, multiple drones, perceive → reason → act, with the person
   deciding in your GUI. **Show at least one extension handled live**: a
   drone going silent, a bad detection, a failed approval.
3. **Evidence** (about 3 minutes): what your testing agent checks, what
   it found, what it missed.
4. **Limitations and lessons** (about 2 minutes): what doesn't work, why,
   and what you'd do differently.

**Questions come from an external panel** of PhD students from the
instructor's research group, who haven't seen your project before, and
from the instructor. Explain your system for someone meeting it for the
first time.
Have a recording ready: if the live run fails, switch to it. You lose the
live-run points but not the rest.

---

### Operator evidence (before Monday, Dec. 14)

Ask **someone outside your team** (a member of another team, a classmate,
a friend) to run your use case in your GUI with no coaching beyond a
one-line briefing. Ask them to think aloud. Say nothing, and write down
where they hesitate, misread or get stuck, and anything they never notice.
This goes on your website. Do it before Dec. 8 if you can, so you can act
on it.

---

### The final website (Monday, Dec. 14)

Your site has been growing since D1. Now make it **tell one story**:
problem → what you set out to do → what you built → how you know it works
→ what you learned. Each page should lead to the next, not read as a pile
of deliverables. Use **screenshots** throughout: your GUI next to your D1
wireframes, the system mid-mission, your oracle results.

| Page | What it needs |
|---|---|
| **Cover (Home)** | Project name, a one-paragraph vision, a hero image or short video of your system running, the team, and where to read next |
| **Vision & Use Cases** | Updated from D1; what changed in your vision and why |
| **Architecture** | The final architecture, ADRs current (superseded ones kept), the GUI screens |
| **Implementation** | What works, what's mocked, what was cut (your D3 scope list, honestly updated) |
| **Verification & Evidence** | Your D4 oracle table with current results, the fault runs and what they revealed, what your agent missed, and the evidence behind your success criteria |
| **Operator evidence** | What happened when someone outside your team ran your use case in the GUI, and what you changed |
| **Native AI** | What engineering work you delegated to AI, what stayed a human responsibility, and how you decided whether the AI's work was trustworthy, with specific examples |
| **Reflection & Limitations** | What you'd do differently, the limitations a real deployment would hit, and anything that never worked |

#### Going public (optional)

Your repo and website stay private unless your team decides otherwise.
After Dec. 14, your team can choose, **unanimously**, one of:

- **Keep it private** (the default).
- **Make your team repo public**, so the website can be published with
  GitHub Pages from `docs/`.
- **Get a new public repo** to stage a showcase website: start from your
  current pages plus a front page that pulls them together, or build it from
  scratch. Your project history and homework-derived code stay private.

Tell the instructor which you'd like. Before anything goes public, remove
`.env` files and API keys, including from the history.

### Before You Submit

Be ready to explain:

- What your system does that it couldn't do on Nov. 19, and what it
  still can't do.
- What happens, step by step, in the extension you showed live.
- Which success criteria you have evidence for, and which you don't.
- One thing you would never have let AI decide in this project, and why.

> **What did you actually build, how well does it work, and what evidence
> supports your claims?**
