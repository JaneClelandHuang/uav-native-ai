Show your architecture working as one system, and find out what happened
to it when it met real code.

  reasoned about, approved by a person, flown, and shown.
- An honest account of what is real and what is still mocked.

  recorded why it changed.
- Defended your system live, against questions about the running code.
- Continuous integration running your tests on every pull request.

**Native AI focus — Intent Is the Bottleneck.** When AI writes much of the
code, the scarce thing is clear intent: what the system should do, and how
you'll know it does. Your D1 success criteria, use case and ADRs are that
intent. This week you check your code against it, and next week your
testing agent starts doing the same.

### What you build

#### 1. A working vertical slice

One real event travels your system end to end, through your real
interfaces: perceived, reasoned about (including any AI component), shown
to a person who approves or rejects it, acted on by the drones, and
displayed. It runs in the simulator.

Mocks are allowed if you **name them**: on screen during the demo, and on
your website. A scripted detection standing in for YOLO is a mock; a
hard-coded "approved" is not a human on the loop.

#### 2. Implementation status

On your website: what works, what is mocked, and what hasn't started,
component by component. Your project board should tell the same story.

#### 3. Architecture: then and now

- Your D2 architecture next to the one you built.
- Each significant change, and why it happened.
- Your ADRs updated: new decisions added, and any decision you reversed
  marked **superseded**, pointing at the ADR that replaced it. Don't
  delete old ADRs.

#### 4. Integration problems and risks

The problems you hit putting components together, what you did about
them, and the risks you still see for the final system.

#### 5. Review outcomes

The most important questions you were asked on Thursday, and what you
changed (or decided not to change) because of them.

#### 6. Continuous integration

Add a GitHub Actions workflow, **through a pull request**, that runs your
tests on every PR. CI runs on GitHub's machines and can't reach your
simulated fleet, so it runs your **unit tests** only. Integration tests
that need the fleet keep running on your own machines, as in HW5.

Use this workflow as `.github/workflows/tests.yml`:

```yaml
name: tests

on:
  pull_request:          # run on every PR
  push:
    branches: [main]     # and again after a merge lands on main

jobs:
  pytest:
    runs-on: ubuntu-latest
    steps:
      - uses: actions/checkout@v4
      - uses: actions/setup-python@v5
        with:
          python-version: "3.12"
      - run: pip install -r requirements.txt pytest
      - run: pytest -m "not integration"
```

It needs two things in your repo:

- A `requirements.txt` at the repo root listing what your own code needs.
- A `pytest.ini` that registers the `integration` marker, and the marker on
  every test that needs the live fleet:

```ini
[pytest]
markers =
    integration: needs the live simulated fleet; skipped in CI
```

```python
import pytest

@pytest.mark.integration
def test_drone_returns_home_after_cancel():
    ...
```

The check must be **green on `main`** by Monday. If your repo is public,
we can also make a passing check required before any PR merges.

#### 7. Git history

As in D2: **at least 2 small, reviewed, merged PRs per team member**
between Tuesday, Nov. 17 and Monday, Nov. 23, each closing an issue and
approved by a teammate other than its author, and **no direct commits to
`main`**.

### Live demo (Thursday, Nov. 19)

**8 minutes of demo, then 5 minutes of live questions**, per team.
**No slides.** The running system is the presentation.

1. Say in one sentence what the slice shows, and name your mocks.
2. Run it: one event, end to end, with the person on the loop making a
   real decision.
3. Show one thing that changed in your architecture since D2, on the
   running system or in the code.

**Questions** come from two places:

- **Your partner team** reads your D2 website before class and brings two
  prepared questions about how your architecture is surviving
  implementation.
- **The instructor** points at parts of the running system: what happens
  if this component dies right now? Where does this message go next? Why
  is this here and not there?

If the live run fails, switch to a recording of your slice running
(have one ready). You lose the live-run points but not the rest.
